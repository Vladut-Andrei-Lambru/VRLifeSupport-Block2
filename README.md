# Virtual Life Support

A hand-tracked VR prototype developed with Virtual Life Support in Utrecht. The brief was to help people who had already completed CPR training feel more confident applying it in an emergency.

[Gameplay](https://youtu.be/tFO7g93U6ew) · [Case study](https://vladut-andrei-lambru.github.io/projects/virtual-life-support/) · [Client](https://virtuallifesupport.eu/)

## What I built

I programmed hand placement, chest compressions, gestures, scenario progression, the live CPR monitor and the results screen. I also helped translate research findings into interactions. This was an eight-week project with five students.

The scenario uses flags to control prerequisites: call 112, request an AED, perform CPR, manage distractions, prepare the chest and follow the AED instructions until the ambulance arrives. Progression is separate from the performance score, which uses compression rhythm, depth and active CPR time.

The in-world monitor gives feedback during the exercise rather than waiting until the final results screen.

## Research and testing

We interviewed CPR trainers and people with practical experience, then tested the prototype with CPR-trained coworkers. Their feedback shaped the scenario, hand placement and guidance.

We added chest drying after noticing that wet or bloody conditions were missing from the client’s existing scenario. Without a CPR manikin, we used a pillow to provide physical resistance during testing.

The prototype received **11/12** and was selected as the strongest of five student prototypes. This is a student training prototype, not a substitute for certified CPR instruction.

## Open in Unity

Use Unity **6000.2.6f2** and open the repository root through Unity Hub. The project targets Meta Quest with hand tracking; testing those interactions requires compatible hardware.
